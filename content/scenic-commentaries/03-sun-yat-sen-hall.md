## English Commentary

### Opening

Good morning, ladies and gentlemen. Welcome to the **Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall** in Guangzhou — a landmark of China’s democratic revolution and a masterpiece of modern Chinese architecture.

### Stop A: Why this hall stands here

Sun Yat-sen was the forerunner of the Chinese bourgeois democratic revolution. He devoted his life to that cause; the **1911 Revolution** he led ended the feudal monarchy that had lasted for thousands of years. To commemorate his contributions, the people of Guangzhou built this hall in **1929–1931** on the original site of the former **presidential house of the South Revolutionary Government**. Construction was completed in **October 1931**.

### Stop B: Sun Yat-sen’s life — the story behind the roof

**Childhood and resolve.** He was born on **November 12, 1866**, in a farmer’s family in **Cuiheng Village**, Xiangshan County — today’s Zhongshan City. At **twelve** he went to Honolulu, where his elder brother sent him to a missionary school. Later he studied Western medicine in Hong Kong and practiced in Guangzhou and Macao. Ever since childhood he was influenced by Western ideas of **Christianity and democracy**, and he made up his mind to cure the ills of old feudal China and turn it into a democratic, strong nation.

**From reform to revolution.** At first he still had illusions about the Qing government and hoped to save the moribund regime through reforms. But China’s defeats by foreign invaders and the corruption and incompetence of the Qing court intensified his patriotic indignation. He decided the Qing was rotten to the core and must be overthrown and replaced by a democratic republic.

**Organizing the struggle.** In **1894** in Honolulu, he founded the first Chinese bourgeois revolutionary organization — the **Society for the Revival of China** (**Xing Zhong Hui**). The following spring he returned and staged the first armed uprising against the Qing in Guangzhou, but it failed. In **1905** in Japan, he founded China’s first political party, the **China Revolutionary League** (**Tong Meng Hui**), later the Nationalist Party (**Guomintang**). After successive attempts, success came with the **Wuchang Uprising** in October **1911**. The Qing fell, and he was elected **provisional president** of the interim government of the Republic of China.

### Stop C: The exterior — Chinese palace, modern bones

The hall is an **octagonal**, palace-like **reinforced concrete** structure, **58 meters** high, with about **12,000 square meters** of floor space. It looks like a traditional Chinese palace in appearance, but was built with the newest techniques of its time.

### Stop D: The architect — Lu Yanzhi

It was designed by a young Chinese architect, **Lu Yanzhi**: born in Tianjin, graduated from Tsinghua (Qinghua) University in Beijing, later studied architecture at **Cornell University** in the USA. He died of lung cancer in **1929**, at only **36**, before the hall was completed.

### Stop E: Inside — 3,238 seats, no blocking pillars

Please walk into the great hall. It seats **3,238** people. From almost any seat you see **no supporting pillar** blocking your view. The huge domed roof is held by **four long-spanned steel trusses**; the **eight pillars** sustaining them are **hidden in the walls**. The acoustics are excellent — no annoying echo — so every speech and performance carries clearly.

### Closing

Remember three layers: the **man** (Cuiheng → Xing Zhong Hui → Tong Meng Hui → 1911), the **shell** (octagon, 58 meters, Lu Yanzhi), and the **hall** (3,238 seats, hidden pillars, perfect sound). That covers both the scenic Q&A and the interpretation passages on this site.

Thank you. I’m ready for your questions.
