## English Commentary

### Opening

Welcome, everyone. Today we visit the **Chen Family Temple**, also called the **Chen Clan Academy** — the **“Pearl of Lingnan Artistic Architecture”** in the heart of Guangzhou. In the family of Lingnan buildings, it stands among the great **ritual / institutional** landmarks — alongside places like the Temple of the South China Sea God and Foshan Ancestral Temple — while Kaiping’s watchtowers represent world-heritage vernacular fortresses.

### Stop A: Three functions, one pearl

This place served as a **shrine** for sacrifices to the common ancestors of the Chen families; as a venue for **clannish activities** on festive and other special occasions; and as a **school** for Chen children — hence “Chen Clan Academy.”

In decoration it gathers the finest Guangdong folk arts and crafts. From roof to floor, inside and out — columns, eaves, ridges, stairs, doors and windows — you will find **stone carvings, brick carvings, lime sculptures, ceramic figurines, wood carvings, and iron-cast** artistic pieces. Motifs carry meaning: the **peony** for richness and wealth, **twin lotus flowers on one stalk** for an affectionate couple, and the **lion** for power and dignity. That is why it is called the Pearl of Lingnan Artistic Architecture. In **1959** it became the **Guangdong Folk Art Museum** (also called Guangzhou Folk Art Museum in English materials).

### Stop 1: Main entrance — drums, “titles and salaries,” brick stories, door-gods

Please stop at the **main entrance**.

**Stone drums** on both sides symbolize social status. In feudal Qing society, a family could place drums before the house only when someone had earned the degree of **jinshi** or higher. Here there is a concrete story: in **1893** — the year before the temple was completed — a clansman named **Chen Botao** won the rank of **tanhua** (third place in the palace examination), and these drums were mounted in his honor.

Please also notice, on the right-hand side **behind the stone drum**, a relief called **“to be conferred a title of nobility”** (*Jue Lu Feng Hou*). A **bird**, a **deer**, a **bee**, and a **monkey** appear together: their Chinese names are homonyms for titles of nobility, official salary, and the granting of noble ranks — a wish that Chen descendants would win scholarly honors and high office.

On the walls beside the gate, **brick carvings** depict different stories from Chinese historical novels — representative works of Guangdong brick-carving.

On the door leaves are **door-gods**. The earliest were legendary **Shentu and Yulei**, said to catch ghosts and protect the house. In fancy armor, rope in hand, they look awe-inspiring.

### Stop 2: “The Advent of Good Fortune”

Notice an inverted character for **happiness / good fortune** (*fu*), often on the reverse of a wood-carving. In Chinese, “inversion” and “advent” are homophones, so an upside-down *fu* means **“the advent of good fortune”** — happiness has arrived.

### Stop 3: Lingnan fruits on the balusters

At the tops of the balusters are carved southern fruits: **peach, star-fruit (carambola), papaya, lychee**, and so on — symbolic **sacrificial offerings** of the choicest Lingnan fruits to the Chen ancestors.

### Stop 4: Hen, chicks, and banana leaves — “great property”

Look at this picture: a hen and chicks foraging under a banana tree. The chickens stand for the present generations; the banana’s **big leaves** (*da ye*) sound like **“great property”** or **“great undertaking,”** so they suggest the wealth and cause created by past generations.

### Stop 5: A picture that encourages lifelong study

Another picture suggests that a young man may learn quickly, but great learning takes years to shape — like the English proverb **“Rome was not built in a day.”** It encourages people to study with **perennial efforts**. In a clan academy, this message fits perfectly: talent needs time.

### Stop 6: The rear hall — Emperor Shun at the top

Finally, the **rear hall**, where the Chen people worshiped their ancestors. Tablets on the shrine were arranged by seniority; at the highest place was **Emperor Shun**, believed to be the remote ancestor of the Chen family, with later generations below.

### Closing

One pearl, three roles; at the gate — drums of **Chen Botao the tanhua**, the **bird-deer-bee-monkey** wish for rank and salary, brick stories and door-gods; inside — six crafts and peony-lotus-lion meanings, inverted fortune, Lingnan fruits, banana “great undertaking,” a picture teaching that learning takes years; and Emperor Shun in the rear hall. That is the full Chen Family Temple toolkit from the white paper.

Thank you very much. I welcome your questions.
